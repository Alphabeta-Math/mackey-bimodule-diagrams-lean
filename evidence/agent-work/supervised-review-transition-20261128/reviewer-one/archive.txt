Bimodule String Diagrams and the
Generalized Mackey Decomposition

Ian Xiao

Abstract

I develop a string-diagram calculus for group-algebra bimodules in which centralizer
cosets record the conjugation embeddings used in induction and restriction. Explicit
inclusion and projection maps give Mackey decomposition for conjugate embeddings. I
prove Reidemeister I and II identities and construct fixed-boundary Reidemeister III
replacements. Some cases require sums, and others admit no replacement even by linear
combinations. For finite parabolic subgroups of S∞, ordered block matching gives explicit
double-coset representatives and Mackey summands. Parabolic intersections and joins
give further identities. For the move with a middle restriction strand, a stabilizer index
determines the attainable orbit coefficients on the right-hand shape over any commutative
coefficient ring.

For fixed endpoints K, H, the objects of DU(K, H) are sequences of strands from H to K,
interpreted as tensor products. When K = H, the empty sequence gives the regular bimodule.
Maps are built from dots, cups, caps, merges, splits, identities, and the associativity and
unit maps for tensor products. We allow tensor product, composition, and finite C-linear
combinations of maps with the same source and target. Equal evaluated maps define the
same morphism. One may also include finite direct sums and matrices of maps. Section 2
uses the full bimodule category for arbitrary direct sums.

1.3
The corresponding functor category

Eilenberg–Watts identifies C[K]-C[H] bimodules with equal scalar actions with C-linear,
right-exact, coproduct-preserving functors from C[H]-Mod to C[K]-Mod. Bimodule maps
correspond to natural transformations.

The category DU(K, H) corresponds to composites of the given inductions and restrictions.
Its transformations are built from the indicated maps by tensor product, composition, and
finite linear combination. For a transformation between two such tensor functors, member-
ship is determined by its component at C[H], using the tensor unit identifications. Finite
direct sums and matrices of maps correspond on both sides.

These composite functors are exact and preserve coproducts. Restriction acts on the same
vector spaces, and C[G] is free as a right C[H]-module along each embedding. Allowing every
bimodule map would give the full subcategory on these functors.

3.3
When one shape can replace another

For shapes A, B with the same boundary shape, write A ⪯B if every labelling of A can
be replaced by a labelling of B with the same boundary groups, boundary cosets, and map.
Internal labels may change. This is inclusion of the sets of maps at each fixed boundary, so
the relation is reflexive and transitive.

Proposition 3.2. Let A be the induction merge with a dot on the right input, and let B be
the induction merge with a dot on the merged strand. Then A ⪯B and B ⪯̸ A.

Proof. The induction-merge slide in (3.1) is defined for every admissible labelling of A and
preserves its boundary and map.

Example 3.2 proves B ⪯̸ A.
